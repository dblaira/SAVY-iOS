Claude’s read of the pile

Six things the 100 show together

  1. Records and surveys disagree on who pays.

    Bank and card records put paying households at 2% to 4.5% (No. 01 to 03). Surveys mostly say 40% to 55% of AI users pay (No. 04 and 06), though one a year earlier said 3% (No. 05). Bank records miss anything billed through an app store or bundled into another product. Surveys count whatever people call paying. The records side with Adam’s first instinct that very few pay.

  2. About half use it, a quarter daily, one in 25 all day.

    In the U.S., 49% use chatbots, 24% daily and 4% almost constantly (No. 07). Other rich countries land close by: the UK at 54%, Australia at 48.6%, South Korea at 44.5%.

  3. Mostly, people ask it things.

    Half of ChatGPT messages are asking (No. 14). Looking things up is the top use in the U.S., Canada and across six countries, and 76% of users in China use it to answer questions.

  4. Many who don’t use it don’t know how.

    In South Korea, 63.5% of non-users say they don’t know how or find it hard to learn. In Brazil, 52% of non-users didn’t know the tools exist.

  5. Logs and surveys disagree on companionship too.

    In chat logs, emotional talk is small: 2.9% of Claude conversations, with companionship under 0.5%. In surveys it is large: 72% of U.S. teens have tried an AI companion.

  6. Age and income split everything.

    Seoul: 63.9% under 55 against 12.2% over. The EU: 64% of ages 16 to 24 against 7% of ages 65 to 74. Brazil: 69% of the richest class against 16% of the poorest.
